# Załoga medyczna i autonomia medyczna w załogowej misji na Marsa: co mówi NASA

Data researchu: 2026-11-14. Notatki po polsku, cytaty w oryginale (EN).
Tagi: **[FAKT]** = potwierdzone dosłownym cytatem ze źródła pierwotnego; **[CZĘŚCIOWE]** = potwierdzone, ale ze starszego/nieobowiązującego dokumentu lub z zastrzeżeniem; **[BRAK]** = nie znaleziono w źródłach pierwotnych.

---

## TL;DR

- **Lekarz na pokładzie: obecnie NIE JEST WYMAGANY (nieokreślone).** Stary standard NASA-STD-3001 Vol 1 Rev A (2014/2015) wymagał dla misji >210 dni opiekuna „at the physician level” (Level of Care V). **Rev B (2022) usunęła poziomy opieki** (Levels of Care), a obowiązująca **Rev C (2023)** mówi wprost: „CMOs often are, but are not required, to be physicians.” Wymaga za to **min. 2 przeszkolonych CMO na pojazd**.
- Starsze dokumenty HRP/ExMC (2017–2019) zakładały „physician astronaut” na Marsie, ale opierały się na Rev A. Nowsze materiały EIMO (2023) przyjmują „Four-person crew (at least 2 crew medical officers)” bez założenia lekarza.
- Na ISS CMO to zwykle nie-lekarz z ~15–40 h szkolenia medycznego; decyzje kliniczne podejmuje Ziemia w czasie niemal rzeczywistym.
- Mars: opóźnienie łączności w jedną stronę do ~22 min, blackout ok. 2 tygodni przy koniunkcji (wg analizy ASCEND nawet do kilku tygodni / miesięcy w zależności od pasma).
- Kierunek NASA: **Earth-Independent Medical Operations (EIMO)** — załoga sama monitoruje, diagnozuje i leczy, wspierana przez system wsparcia decyzji klinicznych (CDSS).

## 1. NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1 (Crew Health)

### 1a. Wersja historyczna: Rev A w/Change 1 (2014/2015) — Level of Care V

Źródło: NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1 Rev A w/Change 1, Approved 07-30-2014, Change 1 02-12-2015, s. 17–18
URL: https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B/Historical/NASA-STD-3001-VOL-1A-Chg1-1.pdf

- **[CZĘŚCIOWE — dokument nieobowiązujący]** Level of Care V wymagał opiekuna na poziomie lekarza:
  > "4.1.6.3 The training and skill of the caregiver shall be at the physician level, because of the exclusively autonomous nature of the mission."
  > "4.1.6.2 Intervention strategies shall be used to reduce the risk to an acceptable level, including increasing levels of autonomous advanced care in the form of medications, equipment, training, or consumables over and above those for previous levels."
  > "Return to Earth is not a viable option for more serious illness/injuries. Impact to overall mission is greater."
  > "4.1.6.5 Level of Care Five shall be provided for lunar/planetary missions greater than 210 days."
- **[CZĘŚCIOWE]** Level of Care IV (dla porównania, ISS i powierzchnia 30–210 dni):
  > "4.1.5.4 Level of Care Four shall be provided for lunar/planetary (destination surface segment) missions greater than 30 days but equal to or less than 210 days, as well as missions in LEO greater than 30 days, e.g., ISS."
- **[CZĘŚCIOWE]** Rev A: min. 2 CMO na załogę:
  > "a. A minimum of two crewmembers per assigned crew shall be trained as Crew Medical Officers (CMOs)."
  > "b. The CMOs shall receive specific training to function as the in-flight medical staff, which focuses on communications during a Private Medical Communication/Conference (PMC), diagnostics procedures, therapeutics procedures, medical equipment, use of the medical checklist, and anticipated medical contingencies."

### 1b. Rev B (2022-01-05) — usunięcie Levels of Care

Źródło: historia zmian w Rev C (s. ~3) — https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/C//nasa-std-3001-vol-1-rev-c-signature.pdf
Rev B PDF: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2022-01-05_nasa-std-3001_vol.1_rev._b_final_draft_with_signature_010522.pdf

- **[FAKT]**
  > "Revision B 2022-01-05 This is a complete revision with the following major changes: Section 3 - The Levels of Care have been replaced with Health and Medical Care Technical requirements."
- **[FAKT]** Potwierdzenie przez NASA ExMC (EIMO, AsMA 2023, slajd 66): "...align with the current 3001 versions (which removed Levels of Care)".
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230007863/downloads/EIMO.AsMA2023.Lemery.final.pdf

### 1c. Rev C (2023-09-15) — wersja obowiązująca

Źródło: NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1 Rev C, "Approved: 2023-09-15, Superseding ... Revision B"
URL: https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/C//nasa-std-3001-vol-1-rev-c-signature.pdf
(Nie znaleziono Vol. 1 Rev D — Rev D istnieje tylko dla Vol. 2. Stan na 2026-11 wg wyszukiwania. [CZĘŚCIOWE])

- **[FAKT] CMO nie musi być lekarzem** (5.2.1, [V1 5003], s. 47):
  > "[V1 5003] Crewmembers who have received a mission assignment as a Crew Medical Officer (CMO) shall be provided with detailed and specific medical training, including but not limited to, health issues, space physiology, behavioral health, medical procedures, medical equipment, toxicology, and countermeasures."
  > "[Rationale: Crew Medical Officer refers to a crewmember who has been assigned as a medical expert for a particular mission. CMOs often are, but are not required, to be physicians. ...]"
- **[FAKT] Min. 2 CMO na pojazd/platformę** (5.2.1.1, [V1 3006], s. 48):
  > "[V1 3006] The program(s) shall train a minimum of two crewmembers per vehicle/platform as Crew Medical Officers (CMOs)."
  > "[Rationale: It is critical to have two CMOs for each vehicle/platform to ensure that a backup is available for medical treatment. ... Additionally, all other crewmembers become familiar with the use of in-mission medical equipment and protocols. This can be further facilitated by choosing equipment that is designed for ease of use.]"
- **[FAKT] Wymagane zdolności medyczne w misji** ([V1 3004], s. 21–22) — m.in.:
  > "h. Assessing, recording, monitoring, and trending vital signs and additional physiological and behavioral health signs."
  > "i. Conducting ancillary tests as needed, including imaging (e.g., cardiac, vascular, ocular), laboratory analyses, and electrocardiography."
  > "a. Medical system architecture and infrastructure (i.e., electronic medical records ...)"
  > "t. Means of providing autonomous medical care and advanced life support."
  > "u. Medical evacuation." / "v. Palliative care."
  > "[Rationale: ... the need for autonomous-from-Earth medical capabilities. In-mission medical care will be in accordance with current U.S. medical care practices with limitations as imposed by mission constraints and managed by the Flight Surgeon, Deputy Flight Surgeon, or their designees.]"
  → To jest najbliższe wymaganiu „monitorowania zdrowia w locie” — **monitorowanie i trendowanie parametrów życiowych jest wymaganiem standardu** (ważne dla naszego MVP).
- **[FAKT] Szkolenie wszystkich astronautów** (Rationale do 5.2, s. 47):
  > "Crewmembers begin medical training, starting their candidate year, to enable them to care for themselves and other crewmembers during missions."
- **[FAKT] Wsparcie decyzji** (Appendix, s. ~80): PRA (IMPACT/IMM) opisane jako "decision support tool to aid clinical stakeholders" — to narzędzie planistyczne, nie pokładowy CDSS.
- **[BRAK]** W Rev C nie ma tabeli „Levels of Care” ani Level V; brak wymogu lekarza; brak wymogu konkretnego pokładowego systemu „medical decision support” (jest tylko ogólne "autonomous medical care").

## 2. Praktyka na ISS (porównanie)

- **[FAKT] ~15 h szkolenia CMO**, decyzje podejmuje zespół naziemny. Źródło: Richardson, Piper, Moomaw, Hailey, "Crew Medical Officer Training and Role of Ground Support for ISS Operations", AsMA 2021 abstract (NASA JSC/KBR)
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205008925 (docx: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205008925/downloads/AsMA%202021%20Abstract%20CMO%20Training_PubPress_DAA_v1.docx)
  > "Pre-mission assignment, NASA astronauts do not complete any medical training, outside of standard first aid and cardiopulmonary resuscitation."
  > "Upon ISS mission assignment, all astronauts begin an intensive training program in the two years leading up to launch, during which approximately 15 hours total is spent on CMO training."
  > "Due to the availability of near-real-time communications between Mission Control and the ISS, clinical decision support for inflight medical and behavioral events is provided by an interdisciplinary team of ground specialists. As a result, CMO training is focused only on familiarization and exposure to spaceflight-specific medical tasks, knowing that ground specialists will provide real-time guidance and troubleshooting in mission."
  > "Medical systems supporting exploration-class missions to the Moon and Mars will require greater inflight medical decision-making autonomy."
- **[FAKT] CMO na ISS = dowolny członek załogi, ~40 h instrukcji** (inne liczenie niż 15 h — różnica źródeł). Źródło: HRP Evidence Report "Risk of Adverse Health Outcomes and Decrements in Performance due to In-Flight Medical Conditions" (Antonsen et al., 2017), s. 34
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170004604/downloads/20170004604.pdf
  > "Presently, a mission's crew medical officer is any individual chosen to be responsible for acute medical care aboard the ISS; this individual may or may not have had any prior medical training or experience (Bridge and Watkins 2011)."
  > "Prior to launch, this medical officer receives approximately 40 hours of instruction in the use of onboard resources and a basic education regarding the presentation of common medical conditions and related superficial treatment options (Bridge and Watkins 2011)."
- **[FAKT]** Levin et al. 2023 (EIMO, IEEE OJEMB, NASA ExMC): CMO otrzymuje "only a few days' worth of medical training before launch"; "Medical data are transmitted to the ground for interpretation." URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10810302/
- **[CZĘŚCIOWE]** Astronauci-lekarze zdarzają się okazjonalnie (np. Auñón-Chancellor), ale nie jest to wymóg — brak oficjalnego cytatu NASA w tym researchu.

## 3. HRP / ExMC / EIMO

### 3a. Ryzyko HRP

- **[FAKT]** Tytuł i warunek akceptowalności ryzyka (EIMO, AsMA 2023, Lemery/Easter/Lehnhardt, ExMC, slajd 3):
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230007863/downloads/EIMO.AsMA2023.Lemery.final.pdf
  > "Risk Title: Risk of Adverse Health Outcomes & Decrements in Performance due to Medical Conditions that occur In Mission"
  > "Risk Statement: Given that medical conditions will occur during human spaceflight missions, there is a possibility of adverse health outcomes & decrements in performance during these missions and for long term health."
  > "What is required for the Medical Risk to be acceptable: High confidence that astronauts can accomplish mission medical tasks in a progressively autonomous fashion (HSRB May 2021)"

### 3b. Evidence Report 2017 (Antonsen et al.) — zakładał lekarza (na bazie Rev A)

URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170004604/downloads/20170004604.pdf (s. 34)
- **[CZĘŚCIOWE — oparte na nieobowiązującym Rev A]**
  > "NASA standards require a designated medical officer, trained to the level of a physician, as part of the onboard astronaut skill mix for planetary missions longer that 210 days given the increased duration, uncertainty and complexity surrounding medical care in this environment (National Aeronautics and Space Administration 2016)."
  > "Physician-level medical training typically takes at least seven dedicated years of medical school and residency training ... This level of training is unrealistic to duplicate within the astronaut training regime; thus, an individual with an appropriate skill set must be selected..."

### 3c. Medical System ConOps for Mars Exploration Mission-11 (HRP 48021, ExMC, kwiecień 2019)

URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200001715/downloads/20200001715.pdf
- **[CZĘŚCIOWE — założenie projektowe oparte na Rev A]** (sekcja 4.5, s. ~17):
  > "Within this mission there is a maximum of four crewmembers. As specified by NASA-STD-3001, at least one crewmember will have physician-level medical training. This document refers to this crewmember, who serves as the primary caregiver, as the "physician astronaut." At least one additional crewmember, who serves as the assistant medical officer, is assumed to have medical proficiency at a level that has yet to be determined."
- **[FAKT] Autonomia** (2.2.2, s. 11):
  > "Although ground medical support will remain an important part of medical care, the autonomous care model for Mars exploration driven by communication delays and blockages requires flight surgeons and other staff to serve as consultants, rather than as direct managers of crew care. The physician astronaut will serve as director of care during real-time medical events and will be the primary source for in-mission medical decision-making. To support the physician astronaut, this medical care paradigm requires comprehensive vehicle capabilities and resources in the form of onboard medical references and decision support systems. Future analysis is required to determine the expected and required levels of autonomy."
- **[FAKT] Świadomość naziemna / monitoring** (2.2.7, s. 12):
  > "Despite the increase in crew autonomy for Mars missions, the ground support system should continue to be informed on the state of the crew and medical system ... Future analysis is required to determine the level and frequency of monitoring and transmission of data to the ground support system to ensure proper medical support."
- **[FAKT]** Tryby: "Autonomous — No expectation or no opportunity for [ground input]"; "Self-Care — Self-treatment by the patient" (Tabela 5-1).

### 3d. EIMO (2023–2025) — nowszy kierunek

- **[FAKT] Założenie załogi bez lekarza** (EIMO AsMA 2023, slajd 69, LDLOLS Foundation — misja księżycowa ~9 mies., ale "PREPARES FOR MARS"):
  > "Crew composition Four-person crew (at least 2 crew medical officers)"
- **[FAKT]** (slajd 18):
  > "Thus, even for non-emergent medical problems, there will be a requirement for high onboard KSA—via a skilled medical practitioner and/or a highly functional CDSS."
  ("and/or" — NASA dopuszcza, że wiedzę zapewni system wsparcia decyzji, nie lekarz.)
- **[FAKT]** (slajd 20):
  > "LEO operations offload many medical decisions, monitoring, and other tasks to ground based support teams"
  > "More advanced systems may even be able to passively monitor crew for early signs of behavioral or medical anomalies, provide diagnostic assistance, autonomously suggest treatments, and guide crew members through procedures."
- **[FAKT]** EIMO ConOps (NTRS 20250000009, 2025, abstrakt):
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250000009
  > "In contrast to the current crew health paradigm for low-Earth orbit and Lunar missions, which depends on real-time communication with Mission Control, deep-space exploration missions will require a significant shift in medical operations."
  > "...known as "Earth-Independent Medical Operations" (EIMO), explores the gradual transfer of medical care and decision-making from Earth-based support to space-based systems."
- **[FAKT]** Levin et al. 2023 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10810302/):
  > "EIMO will require CMOs to perform these functions largely autonomously, substantially increasing the cognitive load."
  > "caring for crew members during communication dropouts will increasingly be required of the crew."

## 4. Opóźnienie łączności Mars–Ziemia

- **[FAKT]** HRP 48021 Mars Medical ConOps (2019), 4.2, s. 13:
  > "Due to the distance between Earth and Mars, unobstructed communication between the crew and ground has a maximum one-way delay of about 22 minutes (about 44 minutes round trip) during the mission. Communication blackouts of 2 weeks are possible every 2 years during certain phases of the mission when Earth is occluded by the sun."
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200001715/downloads/20200001715.pdf
- **[FAKT]** McBrayer, Chai, Judd (NASA Langley), "Communication Delays, Disruptions, and Blackouts for Crewed Mars Missions", ASCEND 2022:
  > "This analysis showed that communication delays will vary from 0 to 22 minutes over the course of a mission, peaking while the crew is at or just departed from Mars. The disruptions occur in a similar time frame and can last for several days up to several months, depending on the solar disturbance sensitivity of the communication band being used."
  Dla misji long-stay: "both propulsion systems experience approximately 13 days of disruptions"; short-stay: 20–21 dni.
  URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220013418/downloads/ASCEND-Communication%20Delays%2C%20Disruptions%2C%20and%20Blackouts%20for%20Crewed%20Mars%20Missions.pdf
- **[CZĘŚCIOWE]** Często cytowane "24 min" — w źródłach NASA tu sprawdzonych pada **~22 min**; nie używać 24.
